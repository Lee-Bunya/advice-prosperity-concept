How it works

Change it once.
It changes everywhere.

All styling lives in assets/site.css. The original build sits at the top. The AP visual design system sits below it and opens with the design tokens: the named sizes, colours and shapes used throughout. Every page shares the one stylesheet.

  • Use a token, not a new number. Photo frames, card corners, label sizes and frame colours each have a token. Adjust the token and every page follows.
  • Placements and copy belong to the build. Styling never moves, removes or reorders a section, heading or tagline.
  • New photo, same frame. Any large photo uses the photo frame below. Do not invent a new shape.
  • Changes go through review. Work on a dana/… branch and open a pull request. The main branch is the live site.

Type
Headings in two colours.The second line carries the point.

Sora throughout. Headings are set tight, in Bluestone with the second line in Brick on light grounds, or in Fleece and Wheat on dark grounds.

On a dark ground

Less admin.More time to grow.

  • Eyebrows (the small uppercase label over a heading) use --label, with a fine line marker: Brick on light grounds, Overcast on dark.
  • Card numbers and chapter labels use --label-lg.
  • One sentence, one line. When a short lead, caption or call to action holds two sentences, the second starts on a new line.
    Give your clients back their adviser.
    Book 30 minutes and we’ll walk a client through the system.
  • Line breaks never change the wording. They are added with a break in the HTML, or a new line in the script for animated captions.

Photo frames

Every large photo
sits in the same frame.

One arched corner turned towards the text, straight edges everywhere else, and a thin line just outside the photo.

The framePhoto starts at --frame-top and runs to the bottom of the section. The thin line sits --frame-gap outside the photo. Where the Arc meets the line, the line stops just behind the Arc’s outer ring.
  • Line colour: --frame-line (Wheat) on light grounds, --frame-line-dark on Bluestone, Overcast on the Brick quote card.
  • Height: photos may run wider to balance a section, but not higher than the frame. Every frame starts at --frame-top.
  • Natural photography only. No overlays, gradients, tints or filters on photos. People across the table, not landscapes or regional imagery.
  • Crop for people. Give faces headroom, centre the main subject in the frame, and crop out stray hands or sleeves at the edges.
The Arc

Anchored to a corner.
Never floating.

Three rings from the brand guide. The Arc sits in a corner of a photo frame or panel: bottom left, bottom right or top right.

  • One Arc per section, sized to the section, roughly 11 to 15 percent of the screen width.
  • Brass to Maple is the lead colourway. Overcast to Workshirt is the cool alternative on Brick or blue grounds.
  • Meeting a frame line: the line ends just behind the outer ring, or the Arc tucks behind the Bluestone edge so it appears to run behind it.
  • On phones the Arcs on photo bands are hidden to keep photos clear.

Navigation

A calm header
that stays out of the way.

The header you see at the top of every page. It is Bluestone over the homepage cover and settles to Fleece as the page scrolls.

  • Links in Sora 500. A fine Brick line sweeps in under a link on hover and stays under the current page.
  • A hairline divider separates the links from Book a demo.
  • Book a demo is always the last item, a round button with an arrow that never wraps.
  • The Product menu opens in the AP card shape, each item marked with a short Brick line.
